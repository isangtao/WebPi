The Photoelectric Effect

When light strikes a metal, energy arrives in indivisible packets — photons. If a single photon carries enough energy to free an electron, the electron is ejected with a maximum kinetic energy set solely by the light's frequency. Einstein's 1905 explanation of this effect turned the "wave" of light into a quantum object — and launched all of quantum physics.

The Phenomenon in Four Facts

01 · THRESHOLD

A frequency cutoff

No electrons emerge below f₀ = φ/h — no matter how intense the light. Wave theory cannot explain this sharp cliff.

02 · ENERGY ↔ FREQUENCY

K_max = hf − φ

Maximum electron energy is strictly linear in frequency; the slope is h. Nothing else enters the equation.

03 · INTENSITY ≠ ENERGY

More light, same speed

Brighter light ejects more electrons per second, but each one carries the same maximum kinetic energy.

04 · INSTANTANEOUS

No thermal lag

Emission begins within ~10⁻⁹ s of illumination. Classical heat models predict a delay that is never observed.

The Governing Equation

E_γ = h·f
Energy of a single photon. In eV: E[eV] = 4.136 × 10⁻¹⁵ · f[Hz].
K_max = h·f − φ
Maximum kinetic energy of an emitted electron, where φ is the metal's work function (the escape price).
If hf < φ, no emission — however bright the source.
e·V₀ = h·f − φ
The stopping potential V₀ is the retarding voltage needed to halt the fastest electrons. A plot of V₀ vs f is a straight line of slope h/e.

Where Photons Do Work

☀

Solar cells

Each absorbed photon liberates an electron–hole pair. The photoelectric effect is literally the engine inside every photovoltaic cell.

◉

Photomultipliers

One photon can cascade into thousands of electrons. Night-vision systems, dark-light detectors, and particle physics rely on this gain.

▤

Photodiodes

Light becomes current: IR remotes, light meters, optical communication, and smoke alarms all count photons as charge.

▦

Image sensors

CCD and CMOS pixels are vast photon counters with single-photon sensitivity — the heart of digital cameras and telescopes.

✶

X-ray photoionization

Hard photons strip inner-shell electrons, enabling X-ray absorption spectroscopy and radiation imaging.

⌖

Quantum metrology

Photon counting, single-photon sources, and photon statistics probe the quantum nature of light itself.
