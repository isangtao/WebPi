The Many-Worlds
Interpretation

Quantum mechanics does not choose. In the Everettian picture the universal wavefunction evolves strictly unitarily — every possible outcome of every measurement is realized, in its own decohered branch of the world. No collapse. No hidden variables. One equation, read literally.

01

The Core Idea

The measurement problem, solved by deletion — not by adding machinery, but by removing the one postulate the formalism never needed.

For four decades after its birth, quantum mechanics was used with a double dynamics: the smooth, deterministic Schrödinger evolution — von Neumann’s “Process 2” — and a second, instantaneous, non-unitary “collapse” (Process 1) triggered whenever a measurement occurred. But the theory never said what counts as a measurement, nor where the cut between the quantum system and the classical apparatus belongs. That gap is the measurement problem.

Hugh Everett III’s 1957 move was radical in exactly one direction: he dropped collapse entirely. Not as a preference — as a consequence. The Schrödinger equation is the only dynamics the formalism contains, so it is applied to everything: system, apparatus, observer, and environment, with no privileged cut. A “measurement” is then just an ordinary entangling interaction, and the state after it is a superposition of observer + outcome terms.

Each term contains a version of the observer who sees a definite result. Because these branches decohere essentially instantaneously, they no longer interfere, and from the inside the theory reproduces every prediction of standard quantum mechanics — while eliminating the measurement problem at the root rather than relocating it.

# measurement = unitary entanglement, nothing more
|Ψ⟩ → a·|↑⟩|observer: “up”⟩|E↑⟩
     + b·|↓⟩|observer: “down”⟩|E↓⟩

# decoherence: ⟨E↑|E↓⟩ → 0, on a 10⁻²⁰ s timescale
# both terms are real. neither is noise. no collapse occurs.

Four Postulates

The entire ontology, compressed. Everything else in the theory — probability, identity, cosmology — is derived or debated from these.

P·01

The universal wavefunction is real

The quantum state of the entire universe is a physically real object in Hilbert space. There is no split between “system” and “observer” — every particle, field, and mind is a component of |Ψ⟩.

P·02

Unitary evolution only

The state always obeys the Schrödinger equation. No collapse, no projection, no special role for measurement. What looks like a random outcome is, from outside the branch, just entanglement unfolding.

P·03

Branching by decoherence

When a system entangles with uncontrolled degrees of freedom, interference between the resulting components is suppressed at rates of ~10²⁰ s⁻¹. The world effectively splits into non-interacting “branches.”

P·04

All branches are equally real

Every term in the superposition exists. The Born weight |a|² is the rational degree of belief an observer should assign to finding themselves in a given branch after the next fork.

03

The Formalism

One equation does all the work. Here is the whole argument in three lines of mathematics.

Eq. 01 — the only law of motion in the theory
iℏ ∂∂t |Ψ(t)⟩ = Ĥ |Ψ(t)⟩

Standard quantum mechanics, as written in every textbook. Everett’s point is that this is complete. The projection postulate of von Neumann’s formalism is not a second law — it is an approximation that becomes exact inside a decohered branch, and nothing more.

Eq. 02 — measurement as entanglement
( a|↑⟩ + b|↓⟩ ) |A₀⟩ |E₀⟩ U — unitary a|↑⟩|A↑⟩|E↑⟩ + b|↓⟩|A↓⟩|E↓⟩

A qubit in superposition meets a ready apparatus A₀ in an environment E₀. The total state evolves linearly: amplitude a flows into a branch where the apparatus — and every observer who looks at it — records ↑; amplitude b flows into a branch recording ↓. Both are present. Neither is “the dream of the other.”

Eq. 03 — decoherence kills the interference, not the branches
ρSA = |a|2|↑A↑⟩⟨↑A↑| + |b|2|↓A↓⟩⟨↓A↓| + ab* ⟨E↓|E↑⟩ |↑A↑⟩⟨↓A↓| + h.c.

Tracing out the environment, the off-diagonal terms are multiplied by ⟨E↓|E↑⟩, which decays to zero as the environment states orthogonalize. For a macroscopic pointer, this happens in ~10⁻²⁰ seconds (Joos–Zeh scale) — faster than any conceivable re-coherence. Decoherence selects the pointer basis (the branches) but does not delete the other terms. Everettianism holds that is not a bug: no single outcome is ever selected, because none needs to be.

06

Key Figures

The people who made it, rejected it, and rebuilt it — plus the critic who keeps it honest.

HE
1930 – 1982 Originator

Hugh Everett III

Air Force officer who wrote “The Relative State Formulation of Quantum Mechanics” as a 1955 PhD thesis. The physics world rejected it for two decades; history mostly vindicated it.

JW
1911 – 2008 Mentor

John A. Wheeler

Everett’s advisor. Called the theory “the theory of branching universes,” wrote the famous commentary, and kept the idea alive in the physics conversation for a generation.

DD
1942 – 2002 Revivalist

David Deutsch

His 1985 quantum-computing paper made many-worlds a working tool for physicists, and his 1998 decision-theoretic argument became the standard internal derivation of the Born rule.

DW
b. 1972 Systematizer

David Wallace

The Emergent Multiverse (2012) is the rigorous reconstruction of Everettian ontology — branch counting, self-locating uncertainty, and decision theory, done properly.

LV
b. 1952 Refiner

Lev Vaidman

Defends a “preferred branching structure” — worlds are the natural units of the theory. Also the author of the infamous “quantum suicide” thought experiment.

MT
b. 1967 Principal critic

Max Tegmark

His 1998 Boltzmann-brain objection — that branch weights over the whole multiverse make predictions trivial or absurd — remains the sharpest standing attack on the view.

07

Timeline

From the birth of the measurement problem to the era when its cleanest solution started to look like the default.

1925 – 1926

Quantum mechanics is born

Matrix mechanics (Heisenberg, Born, Jordan) and wave mechanics (Schrödinger) are shown equivalent. Superposition is in the formalism from day one — and with it, the measurement problem.

1932

von Neumann’s double dynamics

Mathematische Grundlagen formalizes two distinct processes: unitary evolution, and the projection (collapse) postulate applied to measurement. The “Heisenberg cut” enters the literature.

1935

Schrödinger’s cat

Wanted as a reductio of collapse — “a quite trivial stuff” becoming a live cat/dead-cat superposition. Everettians read it differently: the cat is a perfect model of a branch, and both states are real.

1957

Everett’s “Relative State” paper

PhD thesis under Wheeler, published in Rev. Mod. Phys. 29, 454. The community rejects it — Heisenberg reportedly calls it “the many-worlds of madness.” Wheeler’s reported advice: “If you can’t sleep with it, you can’t live with it.”

1970 – 1971

A name, and an independent discovery

David DeWitt coins the term “many-worlds” at a conference in Grenoble (1970). The same year, Hans Zeh independently introduces environment-induced entanglement — what will be called decoherence.

1981 – 1982

Zurek formalizes decoherence

Pointer states, “einselection,” and quantitative decoherence rates. Decoherence becomes the physical mechanism that makes branching a viable story. In 1982, Hugh Everett dies of a heart attack at 51 — ten years before his revival.

1985

Deutsch and quantum computation

Deutsch’s universal quantum computer works most naturally on the Everettian picture, and the Born rule appears as a resource. Many-worlds stops being a philosophical curiosity and becomes a working framework.

1998

The Born rule, derived — and attacked

Deutsch’s decision-theoretic argument (extended by Wallace) shows that a rational agent in a branching universe must act as if |a|² were a probability. The same year, Tegmark publishes his Boltzmann-brain critique.

2012

Wallace: The Emergent Multiverse

Everettian ontology is reconstructed with mathematical rigor: what a “world” is, how to count them, and why self-locating uncertainty is a coherent problem with a unique answer.

2016 →

Something deeply hidden

Carroll’s popular treatment brings the interpretation to the wider physics community. In recent polls of working researchers, the Everettian interpretation consistently ranks at or near the top of the field’s own beliefs about which interpretation is true.

08

Open Problems

The arguments the field still loses sleep over. A candidate for “the” interpretation must survive all six of these.

C·01

The Born rule problem

Why should squared amplitudes be probabilities? The standard answers are Gleason’s theorem (1957), the Deutsch–Wallace decision-theoretic derivation, and Zurek’s envariance. Critics call the circular; proponents reply that it is a derivation of rational belief, not of frequencies — and that no alternative interpretation does better.

Gleason ’57 · Deutsch ’98 · Wallace ’12
C·02

Are branches even countable?

“How many worlds are there?” may be ill-posed: branch number depends on how finely you carve the environment. Vaidman’s preferred branching structure and Wallace’s branch counting try to fix this; Tegmark argues the freedom makes the theory unpredicative once cosmological superpositions are included.

Vaidman ’91 · Wallace ’12 · Tegmark ’98
C·03

Self-locating uncertainty

Before I open the box, after branching, “I” am in every branch. Assigning the right credences to “which branch am I in?” is a new problem standard probability theory doesn’t directly address. Everettian decision theory answers it; whether the answer is unique is still debated.

Wallace ’12 · Lewis ’46 (analogues)
C·04

Decoherence is not isolation

Decoherence makes interference fantastically small, not strictly zero. In principle, a quantum eraser can re-cohere branches — so “worlds” are emergent, approximate structures, not fundamental entities. Defenders accept this; purists of a strict ontology do not.

Zeh ’70 · Zurek ’03 (RMP)
C·05

Empirical equivalence

MWI predicts exactly the same numbers as standard quantum mechanics. For Popperians this is fatal (unfalsifiable); for its advocates it is the point — collapse is an extra unfalsifiable postulate, and MWI is the reading with zero additions. Its status is the status of QM itself.

Popper ’34 vs. Everett ’57
C·06

The price of realism

A literal multiverse is a staggering ontological bill. The Occam counter: one set of equations, one state vector, no collapse, no cut, no hidden variables — and the “many worlds” are read off the mathematics, not added to it. The dispute is fundamentally about what parsimony counts.

Wheeler ’57 · Tegmark ’98 · Carroll ’16
