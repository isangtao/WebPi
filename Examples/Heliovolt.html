The Big Picture

Why push efficiency so hard?

The solar cell is the only major energy device whose cost has fallen roughly 99% since 1976 — but every point of conversion efficiency still matters. Higher efficiency means more power per square metre of roof, per kilogram of payload on a spacecraft, and per dollar of balance-of-system cost.

More power per roof

A 26% cell produces ~18% more energy than a 22% cell in the same space. On space-constrained buildings, that directly cuts module count, racking, and installation cost.

Lower levelized cost

Modules are the smallest cost share of a solar plant. Gains in efficiency reduce module area, inverter load, wiring, and land — shrinking the cost of every kilowatt-hour.

Space & critical missions

In orbit, mass is the true currency. Multi-junction cells deliver the highest power-to-mass ratio of any technology — which is why they power nearly every spacecraft.

The Landscape

Where high efficiency comes from today

Three families dominate the frontier: advanced crystalline silicon, emerging perovskites, and compound multi-junction devices. Each trades material cost, maturity, and ceiling differently.

Silicon · Mature

PERC & TOPCon

Textured silicon with passivated rear contacts and tunnel-oxide layers. TOPCon replaces the aluminum back field with a tunnel oxide and crystalline silicon contact, squeezing out 1–2 extra points.

~26.8%research record, 1 sun
Silicon · Emerging

Heterojunction (HJT)

Amorphous-silicon passivation on both faces of a crystalline wafer. Lower temperature processing, excellent light-induced-stability behavior, and a natural platform for tandems.

~26.9%research record, 1 sun
Hybrid · Breakthrough

Perovskite–Silicon Tandem

A thin perovskite front cell absorbs the blue; silicon collects the red and infrared. Already the fastest-rising technology on the efficiency record chart.

33.9%record, 1 sun
III–V Compound · Frontier

Multi-Junction Solar Cells

Several sub-cells of different materials are stacked and electrically wired in series. Each sub-cell is tuned to a slice of the solar spectrum, so the stack harvests far more of the sun's energy than any single material can — reaching 32.8% at one sun and beyond 47% under concentration. The technology that powers space, concentrating power plants, and the world's efficiency records.

Deep Dive · The Focus

Multi-junction solar cells

A single semiconductor can only harvest part of the solar spectrum: photons too energetic waste their excess as heat, photons too weak pass right through. A multi-junction cell solves this by stacking several sub-cells with different bandgaps, so each slice of the spectrum is absorbed by the material best matched to it. The result is the highest efficiency any photovoltaic technology has achieved.

How a multi-junction cell works

Five principles, in the order a photon experiences them.

01

Spectrum splitting by bandgap

Each sub-cell is a p–n junction made from a material with a specific bandgap. A photon can only be absorbed if its energy E = hc/λ meets or exceeds the bandgap. The top cell has the widest gap, so only the most energetic (shortest-wavelength) photons are absorbed there — the rest pass through untouched, because that material is nearly transparent to longer wavelengths.

02

Sequential absorption

Photons that the top cell lets through reach the middle cell, where the lower bandgap absorbs the next band of the spectrum. Photons surviving both reach the bottom cell, whose narrowest gap catches the infrared tail. Watch the animation: violet light is absorbed high in the stack, orange light in the middle, red light at the bottom.

03

Series connection via tunnel junctions

Sub-cells are joined by tunnel junctions — ultra-thin, heavily doped p⁺/n⁺ regions where carriers cross by quantum-mechanical tunneling with almost no voltage loss. Electrically this is a series string: the top cell's back contact is the middle cell's front contact, and so on.

04

Voltages add — the core advantage

In series, the same current flows through every sub-cell, but the output voltage is the sum: V = V₁ + V₂ + V₃. A triple-junction cell can deliver roughly 3 V from a stack only ~2 µm thick — far beyond what a single junction can reach. Since power = current × voltage, this directly multiplies the usable energy per photon energy.

05

Current matching — the design constraint

Because the sub-cells share one current, the stack's current is capped by the weakest sub-cell. Cell designers therefore tune layer thickness, composition (e.g., In content in InGaP), and the solar spectrum assumed at the site so that all sub-cells produce nearly equal photocurrent — "current matching." Under concentration the spectrum shifts, so cells are re-optimized for the concentrator's optics.

Strengths Why they win

  • Unmatched efficiency — the only technology above 30% at one sun and above 40% with concentration.
  • High power density — 3–5× the power per unit area of silicon, ideal for spacecraft where every gram counts.
  • Radiation & temperature resilience — III–V materials degrade far more slowly in space than silicon or perovskite.
  • Thermal stability — GaAs and Ge keep working where other materials would cook at space or concentrator temperatures.

Limitations The trade-offs

  • Cost — epitaxial III–V growth on GaAs or Ge is far pricier than wafer-based silicon; the technology is only competitive where cost-per-watt is relaxed (space, CPV) or area is tiny.
  • Current matching — one weak sub-cell throttles the whole stack, demanding precise growth and design.
  • Lattice strain — stacking mismatched materials builds mechanical stress; "metamorphic" designs buffer it with graded layers, at the cost of added complexity.
  • Spectrum dependence — a cell matched to AM0 (space) isn't optimal at AM1.5 (ground), and vice versa.

APPLICATION · 01

Space & satellites

Almost every modern spacecraft — from GPS constellations to the James Webb Space Telescope — is powered by triple-junction GaInP/GaAs/Ge arrays, chosen for record power-to-mass and decades of radiation tolerance.

APPLICATION · 02

Concentrating photovoltaics (CPV)

On the ground, lenses or mirrors focus 300–600 suns onto centimeter-scale multi-junction cells. Because cell cost is a small fraction of a CPV plant's cost, the optics pay back the expensive chip — with a tiny land footprint.

APPLICATION · 03

Micro-power & research

Millimeter-scale cells power wearables, IoT sensors, and lab instruments where the simplest, densest, longest-lived source wins. They also serve as the benchmark against which every new photovoltaic material is measured.

☀

The physics takeaway: a multi-junction cell is, in essence, a prism made of semiconductor. By splitting the solar spectrum across stacked bandgaps and summing the voltages of each sub-cell in series, it approaches the thermodynamic limit of concentrated light — the ~86% of the Shockley–Queisser ceiling that no single junction can ever touch.
