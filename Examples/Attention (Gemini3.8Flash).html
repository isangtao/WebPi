Demystifying Query, Key, & Value

Attention gives language models the superpower to dynamically contextualize words. Instead of treating words in isolation, each word asks questions, inspects other words, and extracts the exact information it needs.

Attention( Q, K, V ) = softmax( Q ⋅ KT / √dk) V

1. The Intuitive Analogy: Video Search

Think of attention like searching on YouTube or an archive library. You enter a search query, the database compares it against video metadata tags, and then plays the matching video stream.

QUERY (Q)

What am I looking for?

The prompt or focus vector generated by the current token. It represents what contextual information this token needs to understand its role.

e.g., "bank" asks: "Am I a financial institution or a river slope?"
KEY (K)

What do I offer?

The label or index card carried by every other token in the sentence. It answers whether it has information relevant to what any Query might seek.

e.g., "money" advertises: "I provide finance/monetary context."
VALUE (V)

What content do I transmit?

The actual semantic payload. If a Query matches a Key, this is the information transferred to enrich the original token's embedding.

e.g., "finance" payload is added to the representation of "bank".

3. The 4 Structural Transformations

Where do Q, K, and V come from? They are generated by multiplying the input embedding $X$ with three distinct learned weight matrices: $W_Q, W_K, W_V$.

1

Linear Projection

The raw token vector $X$ is multiplied by weights: Q = X ⋅ WQ, K = X ⋅ WK, and V = X ⋅ WV. This gives the word different representations for questioning, indexing, and communicating.

2

Dot-Product Matching

We take the dot product of Q with all Ks. A higher dot product means the vectors point in similar directions in latent space, indicating high semantic relevance.

3

Scaling & Softmax

We divide by the square root of the dimension ($\sqrt{d_k}$) so large vectors don't saturate the softmax function into binary zero-or-one outputs, preserving fluid gradients.

4

Value Synthesis

Each token's Value vector is multiplied by its softmax score. Adding them produces a new, rich vector that replaces the naive static dictionary definition with full context.

4. How is Attention Statistically Derived from Training Data?

Matrices $W_Q, W_K, W_V$ are not handcrafted by engineers. They start as pure Gaussian noise. Here is how millions of corpus sentences statistically mold them.

Language Statistics & Co-occurrence

When training on millions of articles, the model plays next-token prediction: "Given previous tokens, what comes next?"

If the word "bank" appears near "river", "water", and "flow", next-token prediction requires resolving "bank" as natural geography. The training loss pushes $W_Q$ and $W_K$ via gradient descent to maximize dot products between co-occurring contextual clues.
