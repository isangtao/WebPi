Section 01

The market has quietly changed shape

AI didn't shrink the software job market so much as it re-priced it. Commodity output — boilerplate, CRUD, straightforward features — is now nearly free. What got more expensive: the decisions that output depends on. These four directional signals frame everything that follows.

AI-Generated Output

▲ Rising

Copilots and agents normalize the median code artifact. "Can write code" is now the floor, not the differentiator. Volume of typing carries zero signal in an interview or a résumé.

Bar at Every Level

▲ Rising

Roles that once required a junior now require someone who can direct an AI, verify its output, and own correctness. The entry bar rose; headcount didn't follow.

Senior & Leadership Demand

→ Holds

Companies hiring for leverage: people who scope correctly, de-risk architecture, and ship through ambiguity. Experience with production AI is the new scarcity premium.

AI-Augmented Assessments

▲ Rising

Coding tests increasingly assume tool use (C++ screeners included). The differentiator is verification: reading generated code, spotting the subtle bug, defending the trade-off.

Signals are directional indicators of the 2025–2026 hiring climate, not citations. Treat them as a compass, not a dataset.

Section 02

Seven strategies that still win

Each strategy targets a specific part of the AI-saturated market — with the edge it creates, and how it maps to a senior profile built on systems depth, R&D leadership, and applied AI.

01 / FUNDAMENTALS

Own the hard layer

AI writes code; it still doesn't own correctness. Memory layout, concurrency, performance, and security are the layers where a human must be able to say why it's right. Depth in C++/systems is the moat that generation tools can't erode — it's what makes your verification of AI output credible.

Your edge: two decades of C++ at Nokia, from IM/presence services to certifying 50+ titles on N-Gage, plus evaluating C++ coding tests at CD Projekt Red.
02 / PRODUCT

Ship AI-leveraged products

Model access is a commodity; products are not. The market pays for people who assemble LLMs into working systems — retrieval, tool use, agentic workflows, evaluations, guardrails, voice and vision I/O — and ship them end-to-end. Portfolio artifacts beat résumé adjectives every time.

Your edge: CD Projekt Red's AI stack — story generation, voice cloning, procedural assets & environments, AI-driven physics, motion generation and vision systems, prototyped for next-gen games.
03 / CONSTRAINT

Go where AI is constrained

Cloud LLMs hit walls: air-gapped facilities, regulated data, defense, healthcare, mining operations, sovereign infrastructure. Every wall is a paid niche. Engineers who can deploy secure, local, on-prem LLMs — with the analytics, security, and I/O around them — face far less competition than the API crowd.

Your edge: DataSolve Inc. — custom workflows specializing in air-gapped, secure, local LLMs for analytics, cybersecurity, and voice/vision interfaces. This is a category you already own.
04 / DOMAIN

Stack vertical depth

Generalist AI engineers are saturating; domain-fluent ones aren't. Pairing AI skill with an industry — autonomous mining fleets, gaming engines, transit dispatch, industrial control — makes you the person who can translate a domain's actual problems into AI systems that work there.

Your edge: autonomous vehicle fleet management for mining (Wenco/Hitachi), next-gen games (CDPR), paratransit dispatch (DDS Wireless), and industrial hydraulic/pneumatic control (Wainbee).
05 / PROOF

Prove with artifacts

In a market flooded with AI-polished résumés, verifiable output is the antidote: a deployed demo, a public repo with a real system, a write-up of an architecture decision, a talk. One strong artifact does more than five bullets. Recruiters in 2026 click links before they read paragraphs.

Your edge: community proof already exists — Cloud Summit Vancouver (speakers & community committee), CENGN commercialization work, Imaging the World. Add one public technical artifact and the loop closes.
06 / INTERVIEW

Interview as an engineer

With tools in the room, whiteboard speed means little. The modern loop rewards: scoping the right problem, using AI deliberately, then verifying and critiquing what it produced — and articulating trade-offs aloud. Practice by solving problems with tools enabled and narrating every judgment call.

Your edge: you've been on both sides — authoring and evaluating C++ coding tests at CDPR means you know exactly what interviewers are scanning for.
07 / LANE

Pick your lane deliberately

The market prices four lanes differently in 2026: deep IC, staff/principal, engineering leadership, and founder. The rarest premium is the player-manager — someone who can still touch the architecture while scaling a team. Choose the lane, then make every artifact, headline, and conversation consistent with it.

Your edge: you have all four lanes — C++ developer, AGI researcher, R&D director managing up to 35 engineers, and founder of AGI Labs. The lane choice is a targeting decision, not a limitation.
08 / SEQUENCING

Sequence beats intensity

In a saturated market, order matters more than effort. Pick the lane first, then build the artifact, then target constrained niches, then negotiate from evidence. Sprinkling all four in random order is how strong profiles get lost in the applicant pile.

The roadmap in Section 05 sequences all seven into a 90-day plan.

Section 03

Role playbooks

The same market, four different doors. Expand the role you're targeting — each playbook turns the seven strategies into concrete, week-scale actions for that lane.

Senior / Staff IC — C++, Python, Systems Individual-contributor depth
  • Reframe the headline. Lead with verification and architecture: "Senior systems engineer — AI-augmented C++/Python, performance-critical and secure systems," not years of languages.
  • Build one flagship repo. A local-LLM inference or RL environment harness in C++/Python with a README that explains the hard decisions. This single artifact answers "can you still code?" and "do you understand AI?" at once.
  • Target the constrained niche first. Air-gapped, on-prem, and regulated AI deployments (defense, mining, healthcare, finance) are where senior systems skills still command premium rates — and where your DataSolve work is a direct match.
  • Interview with tools on. Practice 45-minute problems: generate with a copilot, then spend the second half finding its flaws. Narrate the verification — that's the senior signal.
  • Negotiate on scarcity. Staff-level ICs who can own an AI subsystem end-to-end (model → integration → evals → ops) are scarce. Anchor on outcome, not level title.
Head of AI / R&D Manager Technical leadership
  • Sell the operating system, not the org chart. Leadership résumés in 2026 must show how you run AI work: how you scoped models vs. heuristics, gated risk, and kept teams shipping through model churn.
  • Quantify leverage. Teams built from scratch (12, 18, 25 engineers), teams led (up to 35), programs delivered ahead of committed dates — translate these into "capacity created per dollar," the language boards and VPs now speak.
  • Show AI governance muscle. Air-gapped local-LLM deployments, secure workflows, and voice/vision interfaces are proof you can lead AI responsibly under constraints — exactly what regulated industries are hiring for.
  • Target hybrid mandates. The hottest leadership seats are "Head of AI" at non-AI companies (gaming, mining, logistics, health) — firms that have the data and the problem but need someone who's shipped AI before. Your verticals are the shortlist.
  • Prepare the strategy interview. Expect "how would you build an AI practice here?" Prepare a 90-day plan: audit data/constraints, pick one constrained pilot, build the eval harness, then scale. Your CENGN commercialization experience is the story spine.
AI Research & Founder Frontier & venture lane
  • Publish the different thesis. Biologically plausible alternatives to backprop/SGD and self-organizing RL architectures are exactly the kind of contrarian, first-principles work research labs and venture-backed startups fund — but only if the idea is legible. Write it down: one public technical brief beats a private slide deck.
  • Position as a builder-investor. Founder profiles in a saturated AI market win on "shipped + research": proof you can take an idea from lab to production (Nokia → CDPR → DataSolve → AGI Labs is that proof, in order).
  • Court the constrained-application labs. Frontier labs increasingly want researchers who can make models work in the real world — on-device, air-gapped, low-data. Your secure local-LLM experience is a differentiator most pure researchers lack.
  • Use the community as a signal. Cloud Summit Vancouver and CENGN are warm channels for research collaborations and co-founder meetings — convert committee work into two or three deliberate technical conversations per month.
AI-Adjacent Transition Domain experts → AI roles
  • Bridge, don't restart. The fastest transition path is "domain expert who now ships AI," not "junior ML engineer." Keep the domain in the headline; let AI be the vehicle.
  • Learn by deploying, not by course. One end-to-end local-LLM deployment (ingest → retrieval → tool call → eval → guardrails) in the candidate's own domain is worth more than five certificates — and it doubles as the portfolio artifact.
  • Target the vertical's AI budget, not the AI lab's. Every industry is converting its engineering budget toward AI. A mining veteran moving into autonomous-fleet AI has an easier interview than a CS grad applying to the same team.
  • Pair with a senior IC. In a saturated entry market, co-authoring an artifact (a demo, a write-up) with a senior engineer borrows credibility and opens their network — the fastest route past the AI-generated résumé noise.

Section 05

The 90-day roadmap

Sequence beats intensity. Four phases, each building on the last, tuned for a senior profile competing in an AI-saturated market.

Phase 1 · Weeks 1–2

Fix the story

  • Choose the lane from Section 03 (default for this profile: Head of AI / constrained-AI architecture, with Staff IC as the parallel track).
  • Rewrite the headline using the positioning statement — constrained AI, local-LLM security, two decades of systems depth. Cut language-list recitals; the skill wall speaks for itself.
  • Audit the two anchors: the DataSolve air-gapped-LLM paragraph and the CDPR AI-systems paragraph must be unambiguous and specific — they carry the entire differentiation.
  • Align LinkedIn (ca.linkedin.com/in/mcarlos) so the profile, headline, and résumé tell one story.
Phase 2 · Weeks 3–4

Build the artifact

  • One flagship artifact: a public technical brief or live demo on secure local-LLM deployment — evals, guardrails, air-gap constraints. It should take a skeptical CTO under 20 minutes to trust.
  • Pair it with a systems proof: a compact C++/Python repo (RL environment harness, inference guard) that closes the "still hands-on" question.
  • Write the contrarian research note — alternatives to backprop/SGD in one public page. It's the research-lab and venture signal in the same stroke.
Phase 3 · Weeks 5–8

Target the constrained niche

  • List 25 companies with AI + constraint (defense, mining/autonomy, healthcare, finance, energy) where "local, secure, local-LLM" is a live budget line — not 250 AI-lab postings where the profile is one of a thousand.
  • Work the warm channels: Cloud Summit Vancouver committee, CENGN network, and CDPR/Nokia alumni — two deliberate conversations per week, each ending in a concrete next step.
  • Practice the tools-on interview loop: 45-minute problems with a copilot, second half spent verifying and critiquing. Record the reasoning; rehearse narrating it aloud.
Phase 4 · Weeks 9–12

Negotiate from evidence

  • Let the artifacts do the closing: walk each final-round interview from the flagship demo, not the résumé summary.
  • Anchor on scarcity: "secure local-LLM + 20 years C++ + shipped game-studio AI" is a one-person market in most metros. Price accordingly.
  • Keep the founder option warm: if the venture lane opens (constrained-AI infra is a live category), the same artifacts double as the technical due-diligence packet.
